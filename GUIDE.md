# Life OS: a guide for the family

Life OS is our household assistant. It keeps the calendars, shopping, dinners, renewals and kids' chores in one
place, and it can do things for us when we ask. It never spends money or sends a message without one of us
tapping **Approve**.

## Getting it on your phone (5 minutes, once)

1. Make sure **Tailscale** is installed and switched on (it's what lets your phone reach Life OS privately).
2. Open **Chrome** and go to the Life OS address you were given.
3. Tap your name, type the **household key**, tap **Sign in**. You stay signed in.
4. Tap **Install Life OS** (or Chrome menu ⋮ → **Install app**). It now has its own icon.
5. On the Today screen, follow **Getting started**: connect your Google account, turn on notifications.

When you connect Google, you choose whether Life OS may read your email. Calendar and tasks are always
included. Email is only ever read and drafted, never sent without your tap. You can disconnect any time in
Settings.

## Everyday use

| Screen | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| **Today** | What's happening today for everyone, tonight's dinner, anything waiting for your OK, what's due |
| **Ask** | Tell it what you need, by voice (tap **Speak**) or typing: "add nappies to shopping", "what's on Saturday?", "remind me to book the dentist next week", "plan dinners" |
| **To-do** | Everything that needs doing, sorted for you; **Predicted** (what we'll need next); **Week** (the week ahead); projects; Shopping and other lists; dinners; renewals |
| **Home** | One-tap buttons (Leaving home, Goodnight, Vacuum now), the **Leaving home check**, the kids' chores and pocket money |
| **Settings** | Text size, high contrast, read replies aloud, notifications, your Google account |

Things that ask for your OK first, every time: sending any email, unlocking a door, disarming the alarm,
opening the garage or gate, and anything that costs money. Requests to unlock or disarm expire after 15
minutes. You can approve straight from the notification.

## To-dos and projects

Type or say what needs doing in your own words, like "the bath tap is dripping again". Life OS sorts it: which
category, how urgent, roughly how long, the first step, and whether it repeats. The list shows what to do **Now**,
**This week**, **This month**, **Later** and **Someday**. Tick things off with the circle. Repeating jobs come back
by themselves.

For things to buy or book, Life OS does the legwork: a lightbulb comes with the right bulb for that room ready to
add to your Amazon basket, a present comes with three ideas. You tap to add it to the basket and press Buy on
Amazon, then tap **I've ordered it**. If it needs to know something ("What's Mum into?"), it asks once and
remembers.

Need someone in? Just add it: "the kitchen socket has stopped working", "book the MOT", "Leo needs a haircut".
Life OS suggests your usual electrician (or finds local ones), writes the email with three times you're both free,
and you tap **Send**. When they reply you get a notification; the time they agreed is filled in, so it's one tap to
put it in the calendar (and send a quick "see you then"). If they go quiet, it nudges you to chase. Everything
you're waiting on shows on **Today**. Save your go-to people under **Home → Trades and contacts**.

For jobs you'll do yourself, **Find time** offers a free evening or weekend slot and puts it in your calendar.

**Using Gemini instead.** You don't have to open Life OS. Say "Hey Google, add *the oven's not working* to my
to-dos" and within a few minutes it's sorted, with any email to a tradesperson waiting in your Gmail drafts to
send. Ask "what's on my Waiting on list?" to hear who you're waiting for, and "mark ... as done" to tick things
off or to book a time someone has offered. Reminders with a date ("remind me Friday to...") are left alone. In the Google Tasks app, each job's notes show
the next step and any links (like the Amazon basket link), so you can act from there too.

**Handy shortcuts.** The green **+ Add** button works from any screen. Changed your mind after ticking something
off? Tap **Undo**. **More** on a job lets you move it to tomorrow, the weekend or next week, or change it.
**Plan my week** finds free evenings for your own jobs, and **Shopping mode** gives big ticks and keeps the screen
on in the shop.

Want prices from a few firms? Tick **Also ask for a quote from** under the email and they each get it. The job
then shows their replies side by side, and booking one stops the reminders for the others.

**Share it in.** Seen something in Chrome, Gmail or WhatsApp that needs doing? Tap **Share → Life OS** and it opens
ready to add. Long-press the Life OS icon for **Add a job**. No signal? Add or tick things anyway: they're kept on
the phone (a yellow bar shows how many) and go through when you're back.

For bigger jobs, use **Projects**: name it ("Sort out the garage") and Life OS plans the steps. The next step
always shows in your To-do list.

## Things Life OS sees coming

Life OS notices what we buy and do, and how often: loo roll every couple of weeks, bleeding the radiators each
autumn, the dentist every six months, Mum's birthday. **To-do → Predicted** shows what's likely needed next, with
how sure it is and why. Tap **Add** to put it on Shopping or the to-dos, **Not yet** to be asked again next week,
**Not needed** to skip this time, or **Stop predicting** if it's wrong. It learns from every tap.

When it's very sure, it just adds it a little ahead of time (shopping two days before, check-ups a month before)
and sends one notification saying what it added. Anything less certain stays on the Predicted tab with a single
reminder on the day.

It also reads shop receipts in our Gmail (to learn what we buy, and to remind us before a warranty runs out) and
the school's emails (dates go in the calendar, forms become to-dos, "Pack PE kit" appears the night before).

**Ordering the shopping.** Lists → Shopping → **Order online**. Things we usually get from Amazon go into the
Amazon basket in one tap (press Buy on Amazon). For the supermarket, each item has a link to find it, and **Copy
the list** puts the whole list ready to paste into the supermarket's own list search. Supermarkets don't let other
apps fill your basket, so that last tap is yours. Tap **I've ordered everything** afterwards.

**Sunday evening**: To-do → **Week** shows what got done, who we're waiting on, next week's calendar and what's
coming up.

## Money, the car and school holidays

**To-do → Money** shows the subscriptions we pay for and roughly what we spend each month, from receipts in
Gmail. A few days before one renews you'll get "Still using Netflix?". Tap **Keep**, or **Cancel it** for a
reminder with the cancel link.

**To-do → Renewals → Cars**: add the car's registration and its MOT and tax dates are kept for you.

**To-do → Money → Bills** lists the energy, water, council tax and other bills, with the monthly total. If one
goes up you'll get a **Bill gone up** notification with the old and new price. You can keep a photo or the PDF
of a bill with it.

**To-do → Week** shows school holidays and INSET days when we're both working. Paste the school's term dates in
once. For each gap: text someone, find a holiday club, ask for time off, or tap **It's sorted** once it is.

Also on **Week**: **the school run** (who's free for each drop-off and pick-up; tap **Claim it** and it goes in
the family calendar) and **the life admin hour**: three paperwork jobs and a free hour. Tap **Book it**, or
**Another time**.

## Trips and holidays

**To-do → Projects → Plan a trip**: where, when and how. Life OS makes the packing list in Google Tasks (tick it
off there or in Gemini), adds the jobs before we go, puts "Away" in the calendar and warns about passports.

## Dinners and presents

After dinner, Today asks **How was it?** Tap **Loved it** or **Not again**. **Suggest dinners for the gaps** then
fills the week with things we liked and haven't had for a while, and quick ones on busy evenings.

**Home → People and presents**: what each person likes, their sizes and wish list. Present ideas start from
there, and nobody gets the same thing twice.

## The home manual

**Home → Home manual**: where the stopcock is, the fuse box, the boiler, bin days. Anyone can look it up, the
kitchen display shows it, and you can ask Gemini "what's on my Home manual list?". Passwords stay private.

## Leaving the house

Home → **Check before I go** lists anything open, on or unlocked, with a button to sort each one: lock the door,
lights off, heating down. Doors and windows have to be closed by hand. It never unlocks or opens anything.

## The kids

Each child has a card on the Home screen. Tap a chore when it's done (it adds their pocket money), tap **Undo**
if it was a mistake, and **Mark as paid** when you hand the money over. NFC stickers in their rooms can log
chores too.

Weekly pocket money is added every Saturday. Under **Pocket money and saving** each child can save for
something: tap **Put £X towards it** to move what they're owed into it. The kitchen display shows how close they
are.

## Parties, recipes and better prices

**To-do → Projects → Plan a party**: the jobs, a party-day checklist and the calendar entry. Add the guests (with a
parent's email if you have it), tap **Make the invites** and send them from Gmail drafts. Replies are ticked off for
you; tap **Coming**, **Maybe** or **Can't come** for anyone who answers another way.

**To-do → Dinners → Add a dinner from a recipe link**: paste the link, pick the day, and the ingredients go on
Shopping.

When a bill or renewal goes up, tap **Ask for a better price** for what to say on the phone, an email ready in your
Gmail drafts, and where to compare.

On Sunday evening, **To-do → Week → Sunday agenda** has the week and the decisions to make, on one page.

## The kitchen display

The tablet on the wall shows the time, today and tomorrow, dinner, chores, saving goals and what's coming up.
Tap **Ask** and say "what's on tomorrow?" or "what's for dinner?" to hear it. It can only show things; it can't
change anything or see email.

The kids can tick their own chores on it: tap **Chores**, then their name, then what they've done. They can't pay
themselves; we still tap **Mark as paid** on Home.

## Talking to it

On the Google speakers, the phrases we've set up: "Hey Google, what's for dinner?", "what's on today?", "what's
on the shopping list?", "can we leave?" and so on; the answer comes out of the speaker. "Hey Google, add
milk to my to-dos" puts it on the Shopping list. The morning brief is read out in the kitchen at 7:15 on
weekdays. To say anything else to Life OS, use Home Assistant's voice assistant: "ask Life OS to ...".

## Quiet hours

Between 21:30 and 07:00 ordinary notifications wait and arrive as one summary in the morning. Anything urgent
(a water leak) or anything needing your OK still comes straight through.

## Updates

Life OS updates itself. When there's a new version, Settings → **Updates and this computer** shows **Update to
...**. Tap it and Life OS restarts in a few seconds with your settings and history as they were. If the new
version doesn't start properly, it goes back to the old one by itself. If someone sends you an update file,
choose it under **Install an update file**.

## If something isn't working

- **"Can't reach Life OS"**: check Tailscale is switched on, then try again.
- **Google things missing**: Settings → Google accounts → Reconnect.
- **No notifications**: Settings → Notifications → Send a test notification.
- **Words too small**: Settings → Text size → Large or Extra large.
- Anything else: tell whoever set it up; they can run the "doctor" check, which explains what's wrong.

## Privacy

Everything runs on our own machine at home. Your phone only reaches it privately through Tailscale. Your Google
password is never seen by Life OS. Work and patient information is never connected to it.
